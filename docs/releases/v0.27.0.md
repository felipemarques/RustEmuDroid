# RustEmuDroid v0.27.0

**Settings → Sound → Audio output** chooses where Android's sound plays. The first entry, **System default**, is selected for new and existing preferences and shows the device the computer uses now. With it nothing changes: the engine starts with the same arguments and the same environment as in v0.26.1, and Android follows the computer's default output, also when you change it while Android runs. The other entries are the computer's active output devices by their names. A choice is saved in `launcher.json` (`hardware.audio_output` and the device's name) and applies after saving and restarting Android. An older `launcher.json` loads as **System default**, and saving with **System default** writes no new key. The panel of v0.26.0, the engine, the Android images and existing profiles are unchanged.

**On Windows, RustEmuDroid plays the sound itself.** The engine has no option to choose a device, so with a chosen device it starts with its own sound output turned off (`-no-audio`) and the RustEmuDroid window plays Android's sound, which the engine sends it as 48 kHz stereo 16-bit audio over its local control channel, on that device in shared mode. The computer's microphone is unaffected in practice: the engine already gives Android silence instead of the microphone unless host audio input is allowed, and `-no-audio` also turns that input off.

- **Delay.** The window waits for 40 ms of audio before it starts, and keeps 30 to 70 ms waiting while it plays: it repeats or skips one sample frame at a time to stay there, because the engine's clock and the device's drift apart by about a millisecond per second. In the test below, a touch sound reached the device no later than through the engine's own output.
- **The chosen device goes away** (unplugged, disabled, taken in exclusive mode by another program). The window tries it once more at once, which is all a change of its format needs, then plays on the computer's default output and follows that default when you change it: it looks the default up again every second. The chosen device is tried again every 3 seconds and the sound goes back to it when it works. A notice says so once each way, and only after the new device really plays.
- **Nothing can play.** When no output can be opened for 3 seconds, or the engine's audio stream keeps failing for 5 seconds or never starts, a notice says that Android's sound is not playing, why, and that choosing **System default** and restarting Android gives the engine its own sound back. The window keeps trying and says so when the sound is back. No notice is raised while Android shuts down.
- **Where you see it.** The notices use the pop-up of the capture notices, at the bottom right of the window, over Android, also in fullscreen. **Settings → Sound** shows the state of the running session in one line: playing on the chosen device, playing on the default output because the device is unavailable (with the reason), or not playing (with the reason). Every change is also added to **Settings → Launcher output** and printed on the console.
- **Silence.** The device stream starts with the first audible sample and stops 5 seconds after the last one, once the device has played what it was given, so a silent Android does not keep the computer awake or a wireless headset on. Shorter silence is played as it is, so a pause keeps its length. The engine sends nothing while Android's output is idle and zeros while an app keeps a silent track open, so the window decides on the samples, not on the arrival of packets.

If the saved device is not connected when Android starts, Android uses the default output and a notice at the bottom right says which device was missing. It stays for 20 seconds counted from the moment Android is on screen.

**On Linux** the client passes the chosen output of the sound server to the engine through the engine's `QEMU_PA_SINK` variable, and lists the outputs through the sound server's command-line client, only for a chosen device, when Settings opens and for `audio-outputs`. With **System default** saved, starting Android never runs it, and a `QEMU_PA_SINK` you exported yourself still reaches the engine. While the panel starts the engine without a window (`-no-window`, as it does on Linux), the engine has no sound at all; the selector says so for every entry, and the launcher output repeats it for a chosen device.

**Command line.** `rustemudroid audio-outputs` lists the devices with their IDs and marks the system default. `boot --audio-output <ID or name>` chooses one on Linux. An explicit `--audio-output` that cannot be honoured ends with a message and exit code 2 before anything starts: no device has that ID or name (the message lists the devices), the devices cannot be listed, it is combined with `--headless`, or it is used on Windows, where only the RustEmuDroid window plays on another device.

**Where it was verified.** On the Windows test PC with a disposable Android 17 profile, the stock engine 37.3.10 and a virtual audio cable, whose recording end was read back with timestamps (Android rang for an incoming call from its own emulator console):

- **System default:** the engine started with the arguments and environment of v0.26.1 (no `-no-audio`, no sound variable) and owned the only audio session. When the Windows default output was switched to the cable while Android ran, the engine followed it and the cable recorded the ringtone (peak 5273 of 32767); the default was put back afterwards.
- **The cable chosen,** with the engine's surface in the panel (the default) and with **Always copy the frames**: the engine ran with `-no-audio`, the window owned the only audio session, on the cable, and the cable recorded the ringtone (peak 5271 of 32767).
- **The cable disabled while Android rang,** from an unelevated process, as **Disable** in the Windows sound settings does: the sound moved to the default output, and came back to the cable about 1 second after the cable was enabled again. One notice each way.
- **The default output changed while the window played on it:** the sound was on the new default output 0.9 seconds later, and back on the old one 0.8 seconds after the change was undone.
- **Silence:** 8 seconds after the last sound the window's audio session on the cable was inactive. A ringtone that started after more than 6 seconds of silence matched, sample for sample within 7 ms, one that started while the stream still ran.
- **Delay:** from a tap sent to Android to its touch sound on the cable, the median was 296 and 308 ms through the window (two sessions, 20 taps each) and 319 and 367 ms through the engine's own output (two runs of 20 taps in one session). The engine's own delay grew by about 170 ms over 40 seconds of taps; the window's stayed flat. Every figure includes the tap command and Android's touch sound, so the comparison is what was measured; the delay to a real speaker was not.

Not verified: any Linux desktop, a device held in exclusive mode by another program, an unplugged USB or wireless device (the cable was disabled instead), and the computer's power requests (the virtual cable registers none, so the stream stop was read from the audio session state).

**Tests.** The offline tests cover device matching; the boot plan (the default changes neither arguments nor environment, `-no-audio`, messages, fallback); the checks of an explicit `--audio-output` and its exit code; reading the sound server's output list, output larger than a pipe buffer, a hanging and a failing program; an older `launcher.json`, the preference round trip, the labels and the selector text with and without engine sound; the request to the engine; the playback queue, priming, the repeated and skipped frame and the trim; starting and stopping the device stream around silence, with zeros and with no packets; falling back, following the default, returning, and what is announced when; the notice and status texts in the three languages; the notice on screen while Android starts and runs; and the window flag of the engine together with the engine's surface in the panel.
