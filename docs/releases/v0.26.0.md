# RustEmuDroid v0.26.0

On Windows, the panel now shows the engine's own picture: the client takes the engine's render surface into its window, and the engine presents each frame straight to the screen there. No frame is copied into the client, and the client draws nothing for the picture. Nothing has to be turned on. When that does not work or stops working, the panel goes back by itself to copying the frames, the path of v0.25.0, and says why in the boot details. **Settings → Graphics → Android picture in the panel** chooses between the two. Linux, the engine, the Android images, existing profiles and the BlueStacks preview are unchanged.

**What you see.** The picture fills the panel as before, in a window, maximised and in fullscreen, on either monitor. What the client draws over the panel stays on top of the live picture: control hints and in-game control markers, notices, menus, dialogs and the controls editor. The client draws those shapes on the processor and gives them to Windows in a separate window that the pointer goes through. Clicks, touches, keys and the gamepad still go to the client, which sends them to Android as before.

- **FPS counter.** Since the panel processes no frames on this path, **Rust panel** shows Android's own count of new presentations of the foreground game, as **Android game surface** does.
- **Screenshots and recordings.** A screenshot, an MP4 recording, a clip, a GIF and the armed replay buffer still work: the client asks the engine for frames only while one of them needs pixels, and for a moment after Android turns, to learn the new shape of the screen.
- **Fit video frames to the window** only applies while frames are copied.

**The setting.** **Settings → Graphics → Android picture in the panel** (Windows only):

| Choice | What the panel does |
|---|---|
| **Automatic (the engine draws straight into the window)**, the default | Shows the engine's surface whenever it can and copies the frames otherwise. |
| **Always copy the frames** | The path of v0.25.0: shared memory, or the frames inside the messages when the engine does not take the region. |

The choice is saved in `launcher.json` as `panel`; older files load as automatic, and so does a value this client does not know. Changing it restarts Android, like the other boot options. For tests, `RUSTEMUDROID_PANEL=engine-child` or `RUSTEMUDROID_PANEL=frames` overrides the setting when the client starts. With Kemu's engine the panel copies the frames, as before.

**Falling back by itself.** The panel copies the frames, and **Settings → Launcher output** and **Technical details** on the session screen name the reason in the three languages, when:

- no engine window with a render surface appeared within 30 seconds;
- for 10 seconds the engine's window had no visible render surface, or the surface belonged to another thread than the window;
- Windows refused one of the calls involved, with the system's own message;
- the picture on the screen stood still while Android's kept changing. Every 1.5 seconds the client compares a small sample of the panel on the screen with the engine's current frame, and three checks in a row that find the screen still while the engine's frames change make the panel fall back. It checks only while nothing covers the panel and the session is unlocked with the display on. After this reason the panel tries the engine's surface again 60 seconds later, three times at most;
- the engine's window stopped answering for 3 seconds (below).

The line reads `Panel: the engine's render surface, inside the client's window; …` while the engine's surface is used, and `Panel: frames copied from the engine; the engine's surface was not used because …` with the reason otherwise. The line about shared memory of v0.25.0 still names how the copied frames travel. While frames are copied, the engine's own window stays minimised off the desktop, so the engine does not keep presenting a picture nobody sees.

**If the engine hangs.** A thread of its own sends the engine's window a message every 250 ms. When the window has not answered for 3 seconds, that thread gives the surface back to the engine and frees the client from it, and the panel copies the frames, whether or not the client is drawing (minimised, or Home not ready yet). The client's own threads never wait for the engine's window: on the test PC, with the engine's interface thread suspended for 12 seconds, every click and key press reached the client, it kept repainting and it answered Windows within 12 ms.

**The engine's own window.** To keep it out of sight, the client asks the engine, through the profile's `emulator-user.ini`, to open its window far off the desktop and small, and starts it with `-fixed-scale`. The file is written atomically, its other entries are kept, and the window entries it had are put back when frames are copied. A window of the engine that has a title bar, such as a message box, is put in front of the client's window instead of off the desktop.

**Limits.**

- **A flash at boot.** The engine's window still shows for about 0.2 seconds, small, before the engine moves it off the desktop: Qt places a new window in the middle of the screen before the engine applies its saved position. Removing it needs a change in the engine.
- **A connected gamepad** still makes the client repaint 60 times a second to read it, which costs the game's frame rate while control markers are shown. Without a gamepad the client no longer repaints that often; before this version any control scheme did.
- **If the picture looks wrong**, choose **Always copy the frames** and save; Android restarts with the path of v0.25.0.

**Where it was verified.** On the Windows test PC with a disposable Android 17 profile: clicks and touch coordinates with the overlay shown, control markers, a dialog and menus over the moving picture, the controls editor, rotation, screenshot, MP4, clip, GIF and replay buffer, resizing, maximising, fullscreen, the other monitor (driven by the other graphics card), minimising, a window over the panel, a hidden surface caught by the content check, and the engine's interface thread suspended for 6 and for 12 seconds. At 125% display scaling the panel, the overlay, clicks and touch coordinates were right; **150% was only partly checked**.

Not verified on a real engine, only by the offline tests: a message box of the engine put in front of the client, and the content check pausing while the session is locked or switched away and while the display is off. Kemu's engine does not use this path. **Linux is unchanged** and keeps copying the frames.

**Measured.** On November 28, 2026, on the Windows test PC (Ryzen 9 8945HS; GeForce RTX 5070 Ti, connected as an external GPU over Thunderbolt 3), the release candidate of this version, built from its release branch, ran SuperTuxKart 1.5's Black Forest replay at High in uncapped Vulkan in one session alternated with BlueStacks 5, Kemu and LDPlayer 14, with the Best performance power mode and the performance engine. With the engine's surface in the panel it measured 443.7 game FPS at a 1600 × 900 panel and 432.1 maximised, against 364.9 and 330.0 for BlueStacks, 331.1 for Kemu, 302.1 for LDPlayer 14 and 305.1 for the frames-copied path of v0.25.0. BlueStacks and Kemu have one boot per condition in that session, and Kemu's window was not maximised. One game, one replay and one PC. [Full record](../../benchmarks/stk-1.5/windows-rtx-child-window-panel-peers.md) · [Performance history](../../docs/performance-history.md)

**Tests.** The offline tests drive the surface handling against windows of another thread of the test process: taking and giving back the surface without waiting for a stalled thread, a hang handled by the watchdog alone, retries of a stale surface, a surface never destroyed with the window that holds it, and the window classes. They also cover the overlay's drawing, the setting and how older files load, the `emulator-user.ini` entries, and the shared memory panel of v0.25.0, with its fixes made after that release, as the path the panel falls back to.

The Windows and Linux downloads contain the desktop client only. The performance engine is built separately from the published source kit and is not part of the download. [Build the engine](../../experiments/whpx-clock/README.md#4-build-the-performance-engine)
